    ABOUT THIS PROTOTYPE

    About Handsay

    Handsay is an early research prototype for webcam signing, captions and optional speech. It is not ready to serve as an interpreter in a real interview.

    Webcam → body landmarks → isolated sign model → your review → captions / speech

    What runs today

    A third-party model suggests 250 individual ASL signs. You can teach personal gestures to say chosen phrases, edit captions, and optionally share live automatic text in a temporary room. Live captions use stricter score and motion-agreement checks, but errors remain possible. No AI rewrites or invents interview answers.

    Can this device run tracking?

    Check the tracking engine without opening your webcam. This runs a blank test image, not a sign recognition test.

    Webcam tracking

    No wearable sensors are needed. MediaPipe estimates up to 543 face, body and hand landmarks from the camera image. Motion detection groups the frames into a sign. Tracking can jitter or lose a hand, and the model can misread a motion; review the words before sharing them.

    Privacy and limits

    Video and landmark inference stay on this device. Gesture embeddings are saved in this browser. Room text is held in server memory for up to two hours, with up to 200 recent entries. Anyone with an interviewer link can read its captions and send questions. Refreshing this page clears the local conversation and room connection. Browser speech may use your operating system's speech service.

    Research credits

    Model and preprocessing: GestureSign by Shashank Yadav (MIT). Training and sample landmarks: Google / PopSign ASL Signs (CC BY 4.0, as documented upstream). Tracking: MediaPipe. Inference: ONNX Runtime. We did not train this base model.

    The model author reports 72.1% top-1 accuracy on held-out dataset signers. That is an upstream benchmark, not an accuracy measurement for this app or your webcam. Full conversational translation requires further research and co-design with fluent signers.

    Start a conversation

    1. Choose camera or text. For signs, sit in good light with face, shoulders and hands visible. Sign one supported ASL word at a time, use a brief natural pause between words (0.7 seconds by default). Use Finish sign to check sooner. Unclear signs may not be recognized.
    2. Invite your interviewer. Create a link and share it. The header tells you when they connect. Keep your usual interview call open for video.
    3. Compose and send. Recognized words fill Your message. You can edit or type any answer. Send message shows your exact text on the interviewer’s screen. Quick messages send immediately.
    4. Choose a voice if wanted. Use Voice on for sent messages. Settings lets you pick and preview a voice. Read aloud lets you check a message before sending.
    5. Correct or finish. Undo a word, clear the message, or edit it. Optional head controls are in Settings. End session stops the camera and closes the shared room.

    Recognizing text is provisional. Clear words stay on video for 6 seconds; possible words stay for review for 10 seconds, or until the next sign. An unsent single detected word clears after 15 seconds of inactivity. Settings → Finish-pose wait gives you more time. Typed and manually selected messages stay until sent or cleared. For automatic signs, a pause of 2.5 seconds starts a new phrase; personal shortcuts are separate messages. Automatic recognition covers 250 isolated ASL signs, not full sign-language conversation.

    End this session?

    This stops your camera and voice and closes the interviewer link. Your conversation stays on this page so you can download it.
