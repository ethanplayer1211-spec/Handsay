SHORT DESCRIPTION
Handsay helps people with speech difficulties take part in calls, interviews and
everyday conversations. A webcam recognizes supported individual ASL words, while
personal gestures trigger phrases chosen by the user. Captions can be reviewed,
edited and read aloud using a device voice. Typed answers and quick messages
provide a fallback. It is a research prototype, not continuous ASL translation.

PROBLEM
Speaking is often treated as the default in calls and interviews. People who have
difficulty speaking can struggle to join jokes, answer questions and show their
abilities at their own pace. The goal is to offer another way to participate
without requiring everyone else to understand sign language.

METHOD
No wearable sensors: MediaPipe estimates face, body and hand coordinates. Motion
detection groups frames into signs; jitter handling and gap recovery stabilize
capture. The reused GestureSign ONNX model classifies 250 isolated ASL signs.
Personal shortcuts match recorded examples to a chosen sentence. Browser speech
reads the text. We did not train the base model; inference runs locally.

RESULTS / FAILURES
The owner demo shows ASL Hello and a personal gesture producing captions.
On six author-selected upstream test-signer clips, constant Hello matched 1/6
labels and raw model output matched 5/6. Careful filtering accepted 3 correct and
1 wrong caption, holding back 2 correct predictions. We verified pinned model,
clip and test-signer export provenance. The clips were also used during app
work, so these are regression results, not untouched filter or live evaluation.
254 software tests pass. Tracking can be overly sensitive or miss motion;
custom gestures can conflict with ASL. Independent live accuracy is unverified.

NEXT STEPS
Stabilize motion boundaries, reduce personal-gesture/ASL conflicts, and test with
fluent signers and non-sign movements. Improve and verify camera/audio routing
for Zoom, Google Meet and Teams. General call-client compatibility is not proven.
