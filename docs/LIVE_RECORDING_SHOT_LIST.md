# Live footage for the Handsay demo

Record one continuous 30–45 second clip of the actual Handsay window. The goal is
to see a person's movement and the resulting caption together. Do not use the
recorded demo as camera input. No spoken narration or microphone is needed.

## What to record

1. Start Handsay's live camera. Keep the actual input well lit, with face,
   shoulders and both hands in view for tracking. Place the camera securely.
2. Remain neutral for about three seconds. This gives us a non-sign example.
3. Perform your ASL **Hello**, pause naturally, then lower your hands. Leave the
   actual prediction or held-for-review result visible. Do not type the expected
   answer into the caption while presenting it as recognized speech.
4. Choose **ASL + My gestures · shortcuts first** in Settings for the updated build, so ASL and
   personal shortcuts work without switching modes. Wait five seconds after Hello; the next shortcut should appear as a separate message. Check the library for example-quality warnings and replace the weakest take if needed. Keep your already trained shortcut's
   saved phrase/name visible, perform it, then return to neutral. This is a
   personal shortcut demonstration, not general ASL translation.
5. Review the result and press **Show message** or **Send message**. If something
   is wrong, show the correction. Keep errors in the original clip.

If one attempt produces no result, leave it in the clip; one more attempt after
a neutral pause is useful. The raw clip should show what happened without cuts.
Tell us which saved phrase you expected if it is not visible in the recording.

## Recording and privacy

- An OBS **Window Capture** of Handsay can show the real hands and interface
  together. An OBS colour-source rectangle can cover your face in the recorded
  output while leaving Handsay's input unchanged. Keep hands and captions visible.
- The existing **Silhouette** and **Hands only** app modes show coordinate
  drawings, not the real hand image. They are useful private workflow footage,
  but real hand footage is more useful for checking the sign and tracker together.
- A face region can also be masked in the final edit. Only provide footage you
  are comfortable sharing. An identifying face is not required in the final video.
- For Handsay's built-in silent recording, choose **Record my camera with privacy**,
  then **Live captions (accepted words only)** to include accepted words as they
  appear. The default sent-only mode waits for reviewed/sent messages.
- Stop, download the original WebM and attach that file in this chat. An MP4
  screen recording is also suitable. No public upload is needed for editing.

This small demo can establish how those specific attempts behaved. It is not a
held-out accuracy study. A personal gesture learned from the same signer is not
evidence of unrestricted ASL translation.
